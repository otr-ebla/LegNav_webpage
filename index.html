Abstract

Robots navigating among pedestrians typically sense their surroundings with a 2D LiDAR mounted close to the ground[cite: 2]. At that height, the sensor mostly sees moving legs rather than whole people, yet most learning-based navigation methods still treat pedestrians as simple shapes like circles[cite: 2]. This paper addresses that gap with CALF (Convolutional Attention for Leg Features), an end-to-end neural architecture that combines convolutional layers, attention, and MLP to interpret leg motion directly from LiDAR scans and produce safe navigation commands[cite: 2]. The CALF policy is trained using deep reinforcement learning algorithms within LegNav, a custom lightweight 2D simulator that combines 2D LIDAR ray tracing with a novel pedestrian gait model[cite: 2]. The resulting policy is compared against classical and learning-based baselines in terms of navigation performance and social compliance[cite: 2]. The approach is validated through real-world experiments via zero-shot deployment on a TurtleBot 4, yielding smooth and socially compliant trajectories[cite: 2]. Written in JAX, the LegNav simulator enables the training of a deployment-ready CALF policy in under an hour on a single consumer GPU[cite: 2].

Methodology & Framework

The LiDAR Blind Spot

Most mobile robots are equipped with 2D LiDAR sensors mounted between 10 and 20 cm above the ground[cite: 2]. At this height, the horizontal laser plane intersects only the lower legs of nearby pedestrians, missing the shoe protruding below the scan plane[cite: 2]. A standard adult shoe extends 20-30 cm forward from the ankle, creating a significant, undetected obstacle during close-range interactions[cite: 2, 3]. Despite this, most frameworks approximate humans as simple circular agents, completely ignoring leg dynamics and foot geometry[cite: 2].

Non-Slip Gait (NSG) Model

To capture realistic leg dynamics, we propose the Non-Slip Gait (NSG) model, which represents each pedestrian as two alternatively moving feet rather than a single disc[cite: 2]. To avoid unrealistic artifacts like foot-skating, each foot is explicitly anchored to its last touchdown position during the stance phase, and it is free to swing only during the active half-cycle[cite: 2]. The swing-foot target dynamically updates to match the body's forward progress[cite: 2, 5].

LegNav 2D Simulator

The NSG model is embedded in LegNav, a custom lightweight 2D simulation environment implemented entirely in JAX[cite: 2]. In this environment, each pedestrian's footprint is ray-traced individually by the simulated 2D LiDAR, replicating the exact sensor signatures a real robot encounters[cite: 2, 4]. This approach allows the CALF (Convolutional Attention for Leg Features) network to directly map raw sensor measurements to velocity commands while explicitly handling the complex ankle-height LiDAR observations generated by pedestrians[cite: 2].

CALF Network Architecture

Convolutional Attention for Leg Features: End-to-end processing of multi-frame LiDAR scans[cite: 2]

The CALF architecture maps raw temporal LiDAR scans directly to motion commands without requiring explicit pedestrian detection, tracking, or velocity estimation modules[cite: 2].

Key Components:

  • Perception Trunk (1D-CNN): A 3-layer weight-shared 1D-CNN progressively encodes spatial clusters across the temporal LiDAR frames[cite: 2].
  • Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention: A 4-head attention block correlates tokens across the temporal stack to implicitly capture leg motion and dynamic trends[cite: 2].
  • Feature Fusion: Spatiotemporal LiDAR embeddings are concatenated with the robot's goal and kinematic vectors and passed to a 2-layer MLP[cite: 2].
  • Custom RL Heads: The trunk supports adaptable actor and critic heads tailored for PPO, SAC, and TQC training[cite: 2].
